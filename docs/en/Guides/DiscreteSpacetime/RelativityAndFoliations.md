## Abstract

A discrete spacetime carries the relativity of the continuum it approximates: a sprinkling
or a triangulation, which turns a flat or curved spacetime into a finite causal graph; the
Lorentz transformations and boosts relating two states of motion on that graph; the foliations
that cut it into successive moments, each foliation a reference frame; the chains of events an
observer follows and the coordinates they fix; the discrete Cauchy surfaces of a causal set and
the spatial topology a thickened surface presents; the scalar field the causal order alone
propagates, through its retarded Green function, the Pauli-Jordan operator and the
Sorkin-Johnston vacuum; and the general-relativistic constructions, from the metric and its
curvature to the field equations and black hole initial data, carried over to the discrete
setting.
