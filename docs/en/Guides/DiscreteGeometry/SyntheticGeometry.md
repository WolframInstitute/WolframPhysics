## Abstract

Synthetic geometry builds a space out of incidence and betweenness instead of
coordinates, so a hypergraph or a graph can be read directly as a geometry of points
and lines: the incidence structure carried by vertices, edges and hyperedges;
betweenness, collinearity and congruence read off the graph metric; axiom systems,
Tarski's for elementary geometry and Whitehead's for a projective plane, tested on a
finite space; geometric scenes and the constructions made inside them; the simplicial
complexes and combinatorial topology the same data generates; and the planar,
tessellation and configuration structures such constructions live on.
